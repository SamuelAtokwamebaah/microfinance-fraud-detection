# Case memo: officer 11, Ho branch

**For:** the investigator or branch supervisor picking up this queue.
**From:** the detection run of the current portfolio.
**Bottom line:** officer 11 ranks first of 20 and should go to desk review. The
evidence is a pattern across 51 borrowers, not a single bad month, and the
figures below understate it rather than overstate it.

No knowledge of the code is needed to read this. Every number comes from the
detection run and can be checked on the dashboard.

---

## Why officer 11 is at the top

Three independent tests were run on all 20 officers. Officer 11 came **first on
two of them and second on the third**, which is why he outranks everyone. No
single test put him there, and that matters: any one measure can be thrown off by
a difficult book or a bad month, while three agreeing is a pattern.

| What the test asks | Officer 11 | His branch colleagues | His rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| How much of what he was owed did he collect? | 3.2 points below peers, in 9 of 24 months | the branch average | 2nd of 20 |
| How many of his borrowers started paying well and then went patchy? | 9 borrowers, 6.0% of a 150-borrower book | | **1st of 20** |
| How often do his borrowers pay less than the full installment? | **13.46%** of installments | **8.44%** | **1st of 20** |

**The short-payment test is what drove his rank.** It is also the one that
matters most, because it points at the mechanism rather than the symptom. A
borrower who pays something but not the whole installment is the exact footprint
of money collected and only partly entered in the books.

## The figures understate him, and here is why

Officer 11 is measured against the other officers in Ho. **Officer 9, who ranks
second in this queue, is also in Ho.** So officer 9's own short payments are
sitting inside the benchmark that officer 11 is being judged against, pushing
that benchmark up and making officer 11 look closer to normal than he is.

| Officer 11's short-payment rate against | Rate | He is |
|---|---|---|
| the benchmark as the test computes it | 8.44% | 1.59 times his peers |
| his colleagues in Ho excluding officer 9 | 6.51% | **2.07 times his peers** |

Two officers skimming in the same branch partly conceal each other. This is
worth knowing before any interview, for two reasons. The gap is wider than the
dashboard shows, and the two cases should not be worked in isolation.

## What is already documented

The evidence pack on the dashboard holds, for officer 11:

- **51 borrowers** who paid their first eight installments cleanly and then
  began falling short
- **402 individual installments** flagged, each with the amount due, the amount
  recorded and the difference
- **GHS 37,315.59** in recorded shortfall across those installments

The restriction to borrowers with a clean start is deliberate. It is what makes
this a case file rather than a list of everyone in arrears.

## Recommended action by rank

| Rank | Officer | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11 | Immediate desk review. Pull the physical receipt books against the recorded ledger for the 51 borrowers in the evidence pack. |
| 2 | 9 | Interview a sample of borrowers. Same branch as rank 1, so handle both together and do not let one officer learn of the other's review. |
| 3 | 19 | Branch manager discussion before escalating. He is flagged by one test only and may be clean. |
| 4 to 5 | 14, 5 | No action. Note and review next month. |
| 6 and below | | No action. |

The split between rank 3 and rank 2 is where the queue stops being reliable.
Ranks 1 and 2 are flagged by all three tests; rank 3 by one.

## Why ranks 3, 4 and 5 are probably nothing

- **Officer 19** sits third almost entirely on the collection-rate test. On
  short payments he is at 6.5% against peers at 6.32%, which is no difference at
  all. One signal, and the weakest one.
- **Officer 14** collects **better** than his peers, by 0.65 points, and was
  below them in only 1 month of 24. He appears at all because his short-payment
  rate is 1 point above his branch. A book with many market traders produces
  exactly that.
- **Officer 5** fires no signal at all. He is fifth because he is mildly
  mid-table on everything, which is what fifth place looks like in a book of 20
  honest officers and 2 dishonest ones.

## What this memo is not

**It is not proof, and it is not an accusation.** It is a ranked suspicion with
a documented shortfall attached. In the real case this method is modelled on, the
same evidence was the start of an investigation and not the end of one. The
shortfall becomes theft only when a borrower confirms they paid money that was
never entered.

**It also cannot speak for anyone refused a loan.** Everything above is measured
on borrowers who were approved and are on the books. A borrower turned away
before any of this was recorded is invisible to it.
